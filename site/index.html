Plan your day. See how long it really took.

nguda is a tray-first planner and time tracker that shows your real estimate gap. It plans your day from local tasks and Google Calendar, tracks the running task from the status bar, and lets you extend or switch when you run over. Today's summary puts planned time next to actual time, so you learn how far your estimates are off.

Download the latest release

Free and open source. Runs on macOS and Linux.

Features

  • Planned versus actual. Every task has an estimate; nguda records the time you actually spent and shows both side by side.
  • Lives in the status bar. The running task and its elapsed time sit in the menu bar or tray, with Switch and Extend one click away.
  • Recover when you run over. Extend the running task by 5 or 15 minutes, or by any amount, or switch to the next task.
  • Google Calendar in your plan. Events from the calendars you choose are placed in today's plan next to your local tasks.
  • Local first. Your plan, tasks, and time log stay on your computer. There is no account with us and no telemetry.

What nguda reads from Google Calendar and why

Connecting Google Calendar is optional. When you sign in, nguda asks for read-only access and reads only two things:

  • Your calendar list, so you can choose which calendars go into today's plan.
  • Events of the calendars you chose, so nguda can place them in today's plan next to your tasks.

nguda never creates, changes, or deletes anything in your calendar. What it reads is stored only on your computer and is never sent anywhere else. The privacy policy has the details.
